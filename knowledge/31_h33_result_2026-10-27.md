## Why 0.2778 is high, and why a unique file did not beat it

The 0.2778 file is not a new detector. Byte-level work already in this repo showed it is the H19-5 dotted field at spacing 2.8 px after deleting dots within 2 px of the catalogue. Those near-catalogue dots are mostly false-positive echo under a metric that masks known faults and credits a prediction only within 300 m. Deleting them raises DTI without finding a new fault.

That recipe cannot be copied here. The parallel-run rule stops a raster if more than 70% of its dots lie within 3 px of a registry raster, or if rank correlation exceeds 0.90. The 3 px credit kernel and the 3 px duplicate ball are the same radius. Dots that earn credit on faults the family already covers are the dots the uniqueness rule forbids.

A same-day screen, disclosed in the preregistration and not used as the confirmation seeds, measured the gap:

- 60.6% of catalogue pixels are more than 3 px from the d=2.8 dots. That is the only mass a unique detector can score without sitting on the family.
- Those pixels are short and weak-scarp (`step_max` median 45 vs 80) and sit under thicker cover (`depth_to_base_surf` median 396 m vs 233 m).
- A blocked-fold 32-band tree scored AUC 0.787 on the family-covered faults and 0.561 on the uncovered faults. Its top 2% was 73.3% within 3 px of the family and covered only 9.1% of the uncovered faults.
- A uranium-on-thorium residual, the obvious blind-fluid alternative, had miss-vs-background AUC 0.506. It was not selected.

So the bands on hand already explain the faults the family dots. They do not explain the faults the family misses. Forcing the emission outside the registry neighborhood makes a unique file and throws away the credit.
