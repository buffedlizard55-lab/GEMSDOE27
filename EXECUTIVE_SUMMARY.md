## GEMSDOE32: why it may look strong

The measured GEMSDOE32 positive-dot set is the GEMSDOE25 d=2.8 set after removing pixels within two pixels of the known catalogue. Wider spacing and a catalogue collar plausibly reduce redundant or low-yield prediction mass under a distance-weighted metric, but the hidden truth and organizer score-to-file receipt are unavailable. The OWNER-REPORTED 0.2778 cannot be causally explained or independently verified from geometry alone. A genuinely new method might do better, but there is no validated evidence here that it will beat that value. Measured on the pinned peer bytes (evidence/peer_relations_20261028.json): the owner-reported GEMSDOE32 file (37,654 dots) is a strict subset of the GEMSDOE25 d=2.8 field (44,090 dots). All 6,436 removed dots lie within 2 px of a catalogue cell, and the nearest kept dot is 2.236 px away. GEMSDOE31 is the same field with 3,891 dots within 1 px removed; GEMSDOE32 is GEMSDOE31 minus 2,545 dots at 2 px. Owner-reported scores therefore step 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 as the removed collar widens from none to 1 px to 2 px. That is one family with one raster per step and owner-reported values: a dose-response pattern, not proof of cause.
